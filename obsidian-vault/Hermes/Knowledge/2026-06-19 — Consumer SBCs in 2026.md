# Consumer SBCs in 2026

## Summary

Christopher Barnatt (ExplainingComputers) examines the state of play in the consumer single board computer market in 2026. Three years after his last market overview, the landscape has shifted dramatically. While the Raspberry Pi started the consumer SBC revolution in 2012 at $35, the market has matured — and prices have risen sharply. Raspberry Pi became a public company in 2024 and sold 7.6 million units in 2025, yet about 70% of those went to business/industrial customers. The consumer segment faces a perfect storm: DRAM price increases have pushed premium SBCs (16GB Pi 5) toward $300, squeezing hobbyists out. Barnatt outlines three consumer categories (makers, low-cost desktop users, enthusiasts/superfans) and argues each is being priced out differently. Manufacturers are pivoting toward industrial/prosumer margins, while the consumer market shrinks. Barnatt commits to continuing SBC coverage but at a reduced cadence (every 4-5 weeks instead of biweekly), focusing on maker-priced boards under $100, microcontrollers, and innovative architectures (RISC-V, Qualcomm, UFS storage).

## Key Concepts

1. **Three consumer SBC categories** — makers (projects/IoT/robotics), general-purpose desktop users (NAS/media/retro-gaming), and die-hard enthusiasts (collectors/superfans). Each segment faces different pressure points.

2. **The DRAM pricing crisis** — LPDDR memory now accounts for the majority of board cost across all vendors. Raspberry Pi 5 16GB reaches $299.99. This is the single biggest factor reshaping the market.

3. **70/30 industrial shift** — ~70% of Raspberry Pis are now sold to businesses, and ~80% of all SBCs go to industrial/embedded applications, continuing to rise. The consumer market is becoming a side-effect of the industrial market.

4. **Raspberry Pi's public company status** — floated in 2024 as Raspberry Pi plc. The IPO creates pressure to prioritize high-margin industrial sales over low-margin consumer boards.

5. **$80+ entry point for "decent" SBCs** — Once you include a case, power supply, SD card, and accessories, a usable SBC setup now starts at $80-100+. A Pi 5 8GB kit is approaching $200+.

6. **Maker-friendly boards under $100 still exist** — Raspberry Pi 5 2GB ($50), Pi Zero 2 W ($15), older-generation boards, and Pi Pico microcontrollers remain accessible. RISC-V boards and innovative ARM boards with UFS storage are emerging bright spots.

7. **Channel strategy pivot** — ExplainingComputers is shifting from biweekly SBC coverage to every 4-5 weeks, with more focus on microcontrollers (Pi Pico 2 W), innovative lower-cost boards, and RISC-V architectures.

## Takeaway

The consumer SBC market is undergoing a painful but natural maturation. The era of the $35 Pi that could also be a general-purpose desktop is ending — DRAM costs, industrial demand, and shareholder pressure from the Pi IPO are pushing prices into the prosumer range ($100-300+). However, the maker ecosystem isn't dying — it's bifurcating. Budget-conscious makers will use microcontrollers (RP2040, ESP32) and low-cost boards (Pi Zero, 2GB Pi 5), while the high end becomes industrial/professional territory. The creative action is shifting toward RISC-V, Qualcomm-based boards, and UFS storage innovations. The hobbyist SBC market may never return to its $35 heyday, but the learning, tinkering, and community spirit will continue on different hardware.
