## Key Findings

### 1. DRAM Pricing is the Existential Threat to Hobbyist SBCs

Jeff Geerling (April 2026) documents the severity: Raspberry Pi 5 16GB now costs **$299.99** — a shocking price for a board that started at a fraction of that. The price trajectory since launch shows:
- Pi 5 2GB: launched $50 → current $55
- Pi 5 4GB: launched $60 → current $75  
- Pi 5 8GB: launched $80 → current $110
- Pi 5 16GB: $299.99 (the extreme end)

"LPDDR chips now account for the majority of board cost from the vendors I've checked with." Geerling concludes the hobbyist SBC market is "on life support" unless the DRAM situation changes radically. He's shifting to older SBCs, microcontrollers, and sub-$100 projects.

### 2. Market Size & Growth

- 2025 market: **$4.3 billion USD** (Global Market Insights)
- 2035 forecast: $7.3B at **5.7% CAGR** (GMI) / $9.21B at 9.74% CAGR (Business Research Insights)

- Top 5 players hold 46.8% collectively: Raspberry Pi, Advantech, Kontron, AAEON, Digi International

### 3. Market Bifurcation

Two distinct segments emerging:
- **Industrial/professional**: High-margin, expensive boards ($200-600+), long lifecycle, AI/edge compute focus. Qualcomm acquiring Arduino (Oct 2025) and launching Arduino UNO Q hybrid with Linux + edge AI signals this trend.
- **Hobbyist/maker**: Shrinking to sub-$100 boards, microcontrollers, older/used hardware. Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W ($15), Pi Pico 2 W, and budget RISC-V boards are the new entry point.

### 4. Regional Dynamics

- **North America**: 36.7% of market (largest single region)
- **Asia Pacific**: Fastest growing at 6.8% CAGR, driven by China's smart manufacturing and robotics push
- **Europe**: Germany leads with highest industrial robot density

### 5. Competitive Landscape

Qualcomm's entry via Arduino acquisition is a major signal — they're bringing 5G/LTE + edge AI to the maker/educational segment. Radxa is the only vendor maintaining board launch cadence. Niche players like Orange Pi and Khadas continue but face margin pressure from DRAM costs.
